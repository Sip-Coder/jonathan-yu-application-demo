Consent and Comfort

How to choose massage pressure without guessing

Light, medium, and focused pressure mean different things to different bodies. A better intake gives guests language they can actually use.

Pressure Consent Intake

Pressure words are imperfect

One person's medium is another person's too much. The best booking flow does not expect guests to know technical massage language before the first visit.

Rose can ask for comparisons: feather-light, steady and broad, medium with check-ins, or focused only in specific areas. That turns pressure into a conversation.

Consent can be rhythmic

Pressure consent works best when it is repeated naturally. Rose can check at the start, during transitions, before focused work, and any time the body changes.

This keeps the guest from feeling like they missed their chance to speak. It also lets Rose adjust before bracing takes over.

What to notice after pressure changes

After a pressure adjustment, the guest can notice breath, jaw, hands, toes, and whether the body wants to move away. These signals matter.

The session becomes more therapeutic when pressure is measured by reception, not intensity.

Pressure language guests can use

  • Lighter
  • Same
  • More focused
  • Broader
  • Pause
  • Avoid this area
Pressure should never be used to push through pain, numbness, dizziness, or symptoms that feel wrong. Massage should adapt or stop when the body says no.
