Pregnancy Comfort

Postpartum massage as a slow return to your own body

After birth, the body needs care that respects fatigue, feeding posture, scar sensitivity, emotion, and the strange tenderness of being needed constantly.

Postpartum Recovery Gentle return

The body has been working without pause

Postpartum massage should not treat the body like it simply needs to bounce back. It has carried, labored, fed, held, worried, and adapted.

Rose can begin with the practical realities: how long the guest can lie comfortably, whether breasts or belly need space, whether scars are sensitive, and whether the nervous system is near tears or numbness.

Common places that ask for care

Neck, shoulders, wrists, low back, hips, feet, and jaw often carry the invisible work of feeding, rocking, lifting, and sleeping in fragments. Gentle contact can help those areas feel noticed again.

Pressure can stay specific without becoming intense. The guest may need relief, but they may also need not to be touched like a project.

A return plan that is realistic

Aftercare should be small enough for postpartum life. One glass of water, one supported breath, one warm shower, or one stretch at the wall may be more useful than a complicated routine.

Rose can help guests track what felt easier after the session, then use that information to shape the next visit if and when another session is realistic.

Postpartum sessions can focus on

  • Feeding posture
  • Neck and shoulders
  • Wrists and hands
  • Low back and hips
  • Quiet emotional decompression
Postpartum guests with fever, heavy bleeding, new severe pain, infection signs, clotting concerns, incision complications, or mood symptoms that feel unsafe need medical support before massage.
