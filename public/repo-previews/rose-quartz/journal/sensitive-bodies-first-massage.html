Nervous-System Support

Your first massage when you are sensitive to touch

A sensitive guest does not need to be talked into a normal massage. The session should be built around consent, pacing, and choice from the start.

First massage Sensitive touch Consent

Sensitivity is not a problem to overcome

Some bodies respond strongly to pressure, surprise, scent, sound, temperature, or being touched by someone new. That does not make the guest difficult. It gives Rose the information needed to build trust.

A first massage can be shorter, quieter, more guided, or focused only on areas the guest already knows feel safe.

How Rose can lower the stakes

Rose can explain the sequence before beginning, ask permission before changing areas, keep draping predictable, and use a simple pressure language: lighter, same, or more focused.

The guest can keep clothing on for certain styles of care if appropriate, skip any body area, or choose hands, feet, scalp, shoulders, or back only.

Success can be small

For a sensitive guest, success might mean staying present for the session, feeling less guarded in one area, or learning what kind of massage is not overwhelming.

The first appointment does not need to solve everything. It can simply prove that bodywork can be collaborative.

Comfort choices

  • Areas to avoid
  • Pressure language
  • Conversation level
  • Lighting and sound
  • Stop or pause signals
Guests with medical complexity, skin concerns, recent injury, clotting concerns, infection, or trauma symptoms that need clinical support should get appropriate guidance before booking.
