Therapeutic Relief

A posture reset for desk workers that starts with breath

Desk posture is not just a shape. It is a daily rhythm of eyes, jaw, shoulders, ribs, hips, and breath.

Posture Desk work Breath

The desk follows you

Even away from the computer, the body may keep the same shape: forward head, raised shoulders, small breath, tight hips, and a jaw ready for the next notification.

Massage can help interrupt that pattern by working with the whole posture, not only the sore spot.

What Rose can include

A desk reset can include neck, shoulders, upper back, scalp, jaw, forearms, hands, low back, hips, and breath. Rose can begin broad and move into focus as the body allows.

The session should also consider sound and conversation. A guest who spends all day processing information may need quiet more than explanation.

Aftercare at the workstation

The most useful aftercare is something the guest can do at the desk: feet on the floor, one breath before email, shoulders down before a call, or hands unclenched at the keyboard.

Rose can help the guest identify the first body cue that tension is returning. That cue becomes the reset point.

Desk reset areas

  • Neck and scalp
  • Jaw
  • Shoulders
  • Forearms and hands
  • Hips and low back
Persistent pain, numbness, weakness, or symptoms that travel into the arm or hand should be evaluated by a health professional.
