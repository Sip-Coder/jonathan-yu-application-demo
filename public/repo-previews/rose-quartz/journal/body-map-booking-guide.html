How to use the Rose Session Map before you book

A body map helps guests share pain, tenderness, swelling, breath, and pressure preferences without writing a perfect essay.

Body map Request prep Intake

The map lowers the writing burden

Many guests know where the body is asking for care but struggle to describe it in a blank text box. A visual map gives them a starting point.

Rose can receive a clearer summary before confirming the appointment, which makes the session feel more personal from the beginning.

It also protects consent

The map lets guests name areas to focus on and areas to avoid. It can capture pressure, tone, and sensation before anyone is on the table.

That means the first conversation is already more specific than a generic intake.

How to use it well

Choose the areas that matter most today, not every area that has ever hurt. Add sensation words like tight, tender, swollen, bracing, or headache.

If something is medically concerning, use the map to tell Rose and also seek appropriate medical guidance.

Map inputs

  • Body area
  • Sensation
  • Pressure
  • Tone
  • Suggested pathway
The body map is for communication, not diagnosis. New, severe, unexplained, or medically concerning symptoms should be discussed with a health professional.
