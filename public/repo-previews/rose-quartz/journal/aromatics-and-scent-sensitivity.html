Consent and Comfort

Aromatics, scent sensitivity, and the right to choose neutral air

A beautiful massage room should never require a guest to tolerate scent that overwhelms their body.

Scent Aromatics Sensitivity

Scent is part of consent

Spa culture often treats fragrance as automatic. Rose Quartz Massage can feel more refined by doing the opposite: aromatics are optional, gentle, and never assumed.

A neutral room can be just as beautiful. Wood, clean linens, water, warmth, and quiet can carry the atmosphere without scent.

How to offer aromatics carefully

If scent is used, Rose can keep it low, natural, and easy to decline. She can ask before applying anything to skin or diffusing anything in the room.

This matters for migraine-prone guests, pregnant guests, sensitive nervous systems, and anyone who simply wants clean air.

The guest should not have to apologize

The booking flow can make scent preferences normal. A checkbox for neutral room, light aromatics, or no topical fragrance prevents discomfort before it starts.

That small act of choice tells the guest the whole session will be consent-led.

Room options

  • Neutral air
  • No topical fragrance
  • Light aromatics only
  • No diffuser
  • Avoid specific scents
Guests with asthma, migraine, allergies, pregnancy sensitivity, nausea, or scent-triggered symptoms should be able to request a neutral room.
