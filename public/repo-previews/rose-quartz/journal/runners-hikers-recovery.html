Therapeutic Relief

Recovery massage for runners, hikers, and desert legs

Active bodies need massage that respects training load, hydration, heat, feet, hips, and the difference between soreness and injury.

Recovery Legs Active bodies

Recovery is not punishment

Runners and hikers often think recovery massage has to be intense to be useful. But a tired body may need circulation, nervous-system downshift, and broad tissue attention more than aggressive pressure.

Rose can work with legs, hips, feet, low back, shoulders, and breath while respecting the difference between training soreness and injury.

The desert adds context

Las Vegas heat, dry air, long walks, conventions, trails, and travel can all change how the body feels. Hydration, foot care, and pacing matter.

Rose can ask what the body did recently and what it needs to do next. Pre-event and post-event sessions should not be treated the same.

Aftercare for active guests

Aftercare may include water, food, easy movement, avoiding immediate intensity, and noticing whether gait feels different.

If an area feels sharp, unstable, hot, or swollen, the plan should shift from massage to appropriate medical evaluation.

Recovery focus

  • Feet and calves
  • Hips and glutes
  • Low back
  • Shoulders from packs
  • Hydration and pacing notes
Sharp pain, swelling, suspected strain, fracture, heat illness, or symptoms that change gait should be assessed medically before massage.
