Head, Jaw, and Headache Care

Jaw clenching, scalp tension, and the headache map

Headache care works best when the intake maps jaw habits, neck load, screen posture, sleep, and pressure sensitivity together.

Jaw tension Headache Scalp work

The head rarely hurts alone

Jaw clenching, raised shoulders, shallow breath, and screen posture can all show up as head pain or scalp tension. A useful session looks at the pattern rather than chasing one sore point.

Rose can ask where the headache begins, where it travels, what makes it worse, and how much pressure the guest can receive around the face, jaw, temples, neck, and scalp.

Gentle does not mean vague

Focused head and jaw care can include slow scalp contact, temple work, occipital support, upper shoulder softening, and careful attention around the jaw without forcing the mouth or face.

For some guests, the best work is quiet and specific. For others, Rose can use guided check-ins so the guest can name pressure changes before the body starts guarding.

Aftercare for repeat patterns

The aftercare does not need to be dramatic. It can be a jaw check before sleep, screen breaks, water, or a note about when the clenching starts.

The point is to learn the pattern. If Rose knows what returns first, the next session can begin closer to the real source of the problem.

Helpful things to map

  • Jaw clenching or grinding
  • Temple or scalp tenderness
  • Neck and shoulder load
  • Screen posture
  • Light, sound, or scent sensitivity
A new, sudden, severe, unusual, or worsening headache needs medical attention. Massage should not delay care for neurological symptoms, fever, injury, or vision changes.
