60, 90, or 120 minutes: choosing a session length that matches your body

The right length depends on focus, nervous-system pace, complexity, and whether the guest needs transition time as much as table time.

Request prep Session length Planning

A short session can still be complete

Sixty minutes can work well when the goal is focused: head and jaw, lymphatic flow, neck and shoulders, feet, or a clear nervous-system reset.

The key is not to overload the plan. A focused session should protect the main outcome instead of trying to touch everything.

Ninety minutes is the signature middle ground

Ninety minutes gives Rose enough room for intake, positioning, full-body rhythm, and focused work without rushing the return. It is often the best fit for prenatal care or combined specialties.

This length lets the body settle before asking it to receive more specific touch.

When 120 minutes makes sense

A two-hour session is best for ritual care, complex tension patterns, deep rest, extra transitions, or guests who need time to arrive and time to return.

It should still be paced carefully. Spacious does not mean intense; it means the session does not have to hurry.

Quick guide

  • 60 minutes for focused care
  • 90 minutes for signature full-body support
  • 120 minutes for ritual and deep restoration
  • Shorter if the body tires easily
  • Longer when transitions matter
Longer is not automatically better. Medical cautions, fatigue, pregnancy comfort, and nervous-system capacity should shape the appointment length.
