Lymphatic Flow

Massage after surgery: what Rose needs to know before touching scar tissue

Post-surgical care requires medical clearance, timing, infection awareness, and a conservative approach to swelling and sensitivity.

Surgery Scar caution Provider guidance

Timing matters

Post-surgical massage should never be rushed because a guest wants swelling to disappear. Healing tissue has rules, and Rose should know what a clinician has cleared before planning touch.

Sometimes the best care is not touching the surgical area at all. The session can focus on comfort, breath, neck, shoulders, feet, or areas compensating from rest.

Scar tissue is not just texture

A scar may carry numbness, sensitivity, fear, swelling, or a history the guest does not want to discuss. Rose can ask practical questions without demanding the whole story.

If scar work is appropriate, it should be consent-led, conservative, and stopped if the tissue or guest response says no.

What lymphatic support can and cannot promise

Gentle lymphatic-style work may support comfort and a sense of flow, but it should not be sold as a medical fix. Post-surgical swelling belongs inside a medical context.

Rose can protect trust by being clear: provider guidance first, comfort second, pressure last.

Required intake details

  • Surgery date
  • Provider clearance
  • Open or healed incision
  • Swelling pattern
  • Clotting or infection concerns
Recent surgery, infection signs, open wounds, fever, clotting risk, unexplained swelling, or provider restrictions must be handled by medical guidance before massage.
