Five-minute prep

Bring clarity, not a perfect script.

A first visit works best when you share the few details that shape safety and comfort, then let Rose help translate them into a care path.

  1. 01Name the goalRelief, rest, pregnancy comfort, headache care, swelling support, or simply not being rushed.
  2. 02Mark boundariesPressure limits, skipped areas, scent sensitivity, silence, guidance, temperature, or positioning needs.
  3. 03Share cautions earlyPregnancy stage, recent surgery, unusual swelling, infection signs, fever, severe new pain, or provider restrictions.
  4. 04Keep details minimalPrivate notes should stay limited to what helps Rose confirm fit, timing, and care preparation.

Before the visit

The intake is not busywork.

Rose uses the intake to understand goals, body areas, pressure preferences, pregnancy stage, recent surgery, swelling, scent sensitivity, emotional tone, and areas that should be avoided. The goal is to reduce guessing before the session starts.

Share early

Arrival

Start slow enough for the body to orient.

Rose reviews the care focus, confirms what changed since booking, and checks the details that shape comfort: position, pressure, scent, temperature, conversation level, and any areas that need extra care or should be skipped.

Ask for
Quiet, guidance, more time, a position change, bathroom break, skipped area, or a pressure change.
Wear
Comfortable clothing for arrival. Draping and undressing level are discussed privately before table time.

On the table

Consent is a rhythm, not a single question.

You stay professionally draped. Prenatal guests can be side-lying and bolstered. Sensitive-body sessions can stay feather-light. Focused relief can use more precise pressure where requested without turning the whole session into force.

Rose checks
Pressure, breath, bracing, temperature, positioning, and whether the body seems to be settling or defending.
You control
Less pressure, more pressure, no pressure, silence, guidance, skipped areas, and stopping.

Aftercare

The session should end with a practical return plan.

Rose can suggest hydration, pacing, home-care cues, what to notice later, and when a follow-up rhythm might make sense. The recommendation should be useful whether you rebook immediately or simply want to understand how your body responded.

After you request

Rose's reply should make the next step unmistakable.

A premium first visit is not just a calendar slot. After reviewing your request, Rose should either accept the appointment path, adjust the plan with you, ask for provider guidance, or pause care when massage is not the right step that day.

Preview a request
  1. Accept Confirm the care path

    Reply with the session length, focus, rate, arrival or service-area details, contact method, and policy notes Rose has approved.

  2. Adjust Change the plan first

    Shift length, pressure, position, skipped areas, room tone, scent needs, or timing before the appointment is accepted.

  3. Clarify Ask for provider guidance

    Pregnancy, unusual swelling, recent surgery, clotting concerns, infection signs, or new severe symptoms may need medical guidance first.

  4. Pause Protect the guest

    Fever, open wounds, acute illness, unsafe symptoms, or unclear restrictions should pause massage until the safer next step is clear.
