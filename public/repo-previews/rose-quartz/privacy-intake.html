Privacy and intake

Sensitive details should make care safer, not make guests feel exposed.

The Rose Session Map can be a powerful part of the website because it lets guests describe comfort needs before they arrive. This page keeps that experience grounded: collect only what supports the session, keep language plain, and let Rose review every request with human judgment.

Rose Quartz promise Intake should protect consent, context, and dignity.

Guests can share practical care notes without giving a private life story, and AI features should summarize choices rather than diagnose or decide.

What the form should collect

The intake should stay focused on fit for the session.

Rose needs enough context to prepare the room, choose positioning, adjust pressure, and know when medical guidance should come first. The best intake is specific without being invasive.

  • Care focus, body areas, pressure preference, session tone, and comfort boundaries.
  • Relevant cautions such as pregnancy stage, provider guidance, swelling changes, recent surgery, injury, infection, clotting concerns, scent sensitivity, or mobility needs.
  • Preferred communication style: quiet, guided, check-in based, or conversational.
Best input
Plain-language comfort notes Rose can act on before the session.
Good example
Light pressure, left shoulder guarding, no scent, side-lying preferred.
Avoid
Open-ended forms that pressure guests to overshare.

Future AI support

AI can make intake feel guided, but Rose stays the decision-maker.

The revolutionary opportunity is not replacing Rose's intuition. It is helping guests translate body sensations into a calm care summary, then giving Rose a clearer starting point before she confirms the session.

  • AI can draft a session summary from body-map choices, pressure preferences, and written notes.
  • AI can suggest relevant journal articles, aftercare prompts, or questions Rose may want to review.
  • AI should never diagnose, promise outcomes, approve contraindicated care, or override Rose's judgment.
Helpful
Summaries, reminders, care-path matching, and guest education.
Human review
Rose confirms fit, safety, timing, pressure, and boundaries.
Not allowed
Diagnosis, medical clearance, automated approval, or fear-based advice.

Guest choice

Guests should be able to share less and still receive care.

Some people arrive with grief, stress, body shame, trauma history, medical fatigue, or a simple desire not to explain everything. The site should make room for that by separating request basics from optional comfort context.

  • Name and contact can be added when the guest is ready to send the request through Rose's confirmed contact method.
  • Body-map and written notes should help with preparation, but guests can keep them brief.
  • Guests can ask Rose to skip a body area, avoid scent, reduce talking, change pressure, or pause at any time.
Minimum
Contact details, desired session, and enough safety context to determine fit.
Optional
Energy, emotional tone, preferred aftercare, and deeper body story.
Always
Consent can change before and during care.

Before sending details

Preview the care draft first, then add contact details only when Rose's approved contact method or secure request workflow is available.

Before saving preferences

Ask explicit consent before remembering pressure, scent, body-map, pregnancy, or accessibility preferences for future visits.

Before using AI

Tell guests when AI helps summarize intake and keep Rose's review visible in the confirmation process.
